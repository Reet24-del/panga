Panga

Know what you're agreeing to.

An AI deal room for freelancers that answers one question before they reply to a client: Lena hai, negotiate karna hai, ya chhodna hai?

Take it Change the deal Walk away
01

Summary

Freelance web designers and developers quote fixed prices from vague briefs, then discover the real work after they have said yes. Panga reads the brief and client messages, finds the hidden work with evidence, calculates what the deal pays per hour, and lets the freelancer rehearse the hard conversation against a voice-simulated client before they send a quote.

Signature mode

🔥 Client From Hell

The proposed project becomes a live voice call with a simulated client who pushes on every gap in the brief. Afterwards, Panga replays the call and highlights what the freelancer accidentally promised.

Output

A quote you can defend

Three options (a smaller package inside the budget, a full-scope price, a polite decline) and a draft reply. Nothing is sent without the freelancer's approval.

Demo hook

One line for the post

"I built Panga so freelancers can meet their client from hell before signing the deal."

Why open-source AI matters here

Client briefs contain private business details. Panga runs Gemma, an open-weight model, on DigitalOcean infrastructure the builder controls, so briefs are not sent to a closed model vendor. It also keeps the cost of a deal review low enough that a per-deal price can work for freelancers earning in rupees, and the model can be swapped or fine-tuned for the narrow job of reading briefs.

02

Problem & user

The problem

  • Hidden work. "A simple online store" turns out to include migrating 200 products, payment setup, mobile layouts and "small updates later". These details are often scattered across WhatsApp messages, not in one brief.
  • Quoting by feel. Freelancers rarely convert a fixed price into an effective hourly rate, so they don't see that a deal pays below their minimum.
  • Saying yes on the call. Most scope creep is agreed to verbally under pressure ("haan, woh bhi ho jayega"). There is no safe place to practise saying no.

Primary user

Persona (illustrative, to be replaced by real interviews)

Riya, freelance Shopify and WordPress developer, Pune

Sells websites at fixed prices between ₹10,000 and ₹80,000. Finds clients through Instagram, referrals and freelance platforms. Gets briefs as PDFs, voice notes and long WhatsApp threads. Has a rough minimum hourly rate in her head but doesn't calculate it per deal. Dreads discovery calls where the client asks "that's included, right?"

Who pays (hypothesis to validate)

Freelancers who sell fixed-price websites and struggle with quoting, discovery calls or scope boundaries. Two price models to test with real freelancers: a per-deal review and a monthly allowance of reviews. Willingness to pay is treated as unproven until at least five freelancers have used Panga on a real deal.

03

Goals & non-goals

Goals
  • One complete journey: brief → evidence-backed scope → editable economics → voice rehearsal → revised proposal.
  • Every extracted requirement links to the exact client text it came from.
  • Every number shows its inputs, and the freelancer can edit them.
  • The freelancer leaves with a draft reply they would actually send.
  • Deployed on DigitalOcean with traces that prove it works.
Non-goals
  • No "bad client" score or judgement of the client as a person. Panga evaluates the work and the economics.
  • No automatic sending of emails or messages.
  • No contracts, invoicing or payment collection.
  • No support for hourly-billed or retainer-only work in v1.
  • No claim of predictive accuracy from synthetic data.

06

Economics engine

The economics are deterministic code with unit tests. The model never produces a number the freelancer sees. It can only suggest hours, which the freelancer accepts or edits.

total_hours     = Σ hours(requirement) + comms_hours + revision_rounds × hours_per_round
effective_rate  = offered_price ÷ total_hours
floor_price     = total_hours × minimum_rate
gap_to_floor    = floor_price − offered_price
scenario_rate   = offered_price ÷ (total_hours + extra_rounds × hours_per_round + extra_update_hours)

07

Client From Hell mode

The voice rehearsal is the feature people will remember. It has to be grounded, fair and clearly fictional.

"Uploading the 200 products is included, right? And my team can request changes after launch, na?"Hypothetical · from brief lines 4 and 11

How the simulated client is built

  • Persona prompt built from the dossier: the offered price, each requirement with its quote, each unanswered question and the selected difficulty.
  • Pressure plan: before the call, Mastra's client-simulator agent ranks the three to five gaps worth pushing on (ambiguous scope, unbounded support, deadline pressure, price anchoring). The persona works through them during the call.
  • Hard rules: the simulated client may not invent requirements absent from the brief, may not insult the freelancer, and must stay in character. When the freelancer clearly holds a boundary twice, the client concedes. Rehearsal should feel winnable.
  • Voice: an ElevenLabs Agent whose language model is set to "Custom LLM" pointing at Panga's own OpenAI-compatible endpoint on DigitalOcean, which calls Gemma with the persona. ElevenLabs handles speech recognition, turn-taking and the voice; Gemma decides what the client says.

Replay and commitment detection

After the call, the transcript is analysed by the scope-analyst agent. Each freelancer statement that agrees to work, time or money is highlighted:

Freelancer saidClassified asSuggested fix
"Haan, products upload kar denge."Ambiguous commitmentAdd "Migration of up to 200 products" to Option B, or exclude it in Option A.
"Small changes are fine."UnboundedReplace with "2 revision rounds; later changes billed at ₹700/h".
"Launch in two weeks should work."Time commitmentTie the date to receiving products and content by day 3.
08

Trust rules

These rules are product requirements, not guidelines. Each one has a check in the test suite.

  1. Every requirement has evidence. A requirement without a verbatim source quote is shown as "inferred" and cannot be priced until the freelancer confirms it.
  2. Every number has visible inputs. Click any figure to see its formula and inputs. The model never computes money.
  3. Simulated objections are labelled hypothetical. In the call UI, the replay and the export.
  4. Evaluate the work, not the person. No client risk scores, no labels like "red flag client". Language describes scope and economics.
  5. The freelancer approves everything. Panga drafts; the freelancer sends from their own channel.
  6. Client data stays deletable. One action deletes a deal's files, embeddings and transcripts. Briefs are not used for model training unless the freelancer opts in.

Separation of responsibilities

Mastra vs Temporal

Mastra runs agents: prompts, tools, reasoning steps inside one activity. Temporal owns the deal's durable state: which stage it is in, retries of failed activities, and waiting for the freelancer's approval for hours or days.

Use the Temporal TypeScript SDK directly with Mastra agents called inside activities. Mastra's own Temporal package is marked experimental.

Tiger Data vs Backboard

Tiger Data stores searchable project evidence: past scopes, requirement embeddings, actual-vs-estimated hours, lessons. Backboard remembers the freelancer: minimum rate, tone, things they never include, corrections they made.

Stack

  • Language: TypeScript throughout (Next.js app, Mastra, Temporal TypeScript SDK).
  • Model: Gemma 4 through DigitalOcean Serverless Inference, which is OpenAI-compatible. Stretch: the same model on a GPU Droplet to show fully self-hosted inference. Confirm the exact model ID from /v1/models before building.
  • Structured output: JSON schema with Zod validation and one repair retry. Evidence quotes are verified by exact string match against the source.
  • Embeddings: an embedding model available on DigitalOcean inference (verify), stored in pgvector.
  • Temporal: Temporal Cloud if credits allow, otherwise the Temporal dev server on a DigitalOcean Droplet.

Success metrics

Build quality (by submission)
  • Full journey completes on the live URL in under 10 minutes.
  • 100% of shown requirements have an evidence quote that matches the source exactly.
  • Extraction recall of at least 80% on 10 hand-labelled briefs.
  • Client voice response time under about 2 seconds at the median, measured in Sentry.
  • Economics unit tests pass for all formulas.
Validation (first users)
  • At least 1 real freelancer runs a real deal before submission (bonus points).
  • At least 5 freelancers within two weeks after submission.
  • Share who change their quote after a review.
  • Answers to: "Would you pay for this per deal, monthly, or not at all?"
